[slide4] 项目背景以及需求 – EMPACT.SG 凯德希望基金 (CHF) 是凯德集团的公益分支，成立于 2005 年。凯德希望基金根植于凯德业务所在地，以增强社会韧性为宗旨，支持与儿童、青少年和年长者相关的教育、健康和福祉的公益项目。除资金和物资捐赠外，凯德希望基金也积极倡导志愿服务，以构建一个充满关爱及包容性的社区。 CHF 希望在未来加强对学生个体的资助，从心理健康扶助的角度，选取那些对未来规划充满迷茫，对适应环境有焦虑，来自于边远地区的已获受助大学生进行专题的资助活动，帮助他们实现以下目标： 了解自我对未来职业与生活的期待，学习如何制定对未来的规划 了解与掌握为适应未来生活所需要具备的技能与达成途径 建立规划导师进行持续的一对一支持（ Optional ） CHF 准备选择一位咨询伙伴来支持设计并实现如下需求 : 设计大学生资助活动，包括： 通过问卷，调研凯德已资助大学生针对未来规划以及环境适应的困惑，以及相应期待 设计 2025 年以及 2026 年两年的暑期受助大学生活动，第一期活动在 2025 年 7 月暑期于 XX 进行 设计项目跟踪模板，以追踪项目实施目标的达成情况 参与对象为凯德已经在中国资助的 40 多名大学生，首期活动时间预计为一周，在 XX 城市实施。 项目整体预算为 25 万元人民币。

[slide7] 我们对于大学生生涯规划项目的观点   我们相信参与者能够 适应他的职业目标 ， 因此项目应支持参与者设计他们的职业规划，使其完全符合目标企业的要求。 我们相信赋予参与者 思考、反思、实践和应用知识的能力，能够实现有效学习 。 我们相信在设计和实施项目时， 了解参与者的需求 是至关重要的。 参与者作为职场中的新手 1 通过体验式学习实现有效学习 2 基于需求的学习体验 3 我们相信 有效的监测流程能够 确保向资助企业传递参与者的真实感受，从而支持积极反馈 ，进一步落实资助企业对参与者的帮助。 有效的监测以确保成功 4 当设计大学生生涯规划项目时， Empact 会以如下方法论构建方案：
[slide8] 设计与交付大学生生涯项目的主要步骤 举行 1 次启动会议 ，以最终确定工作范围和交付成果，例如学习旅程、对话和培训课程。 制定资格标准 ，以支持对项目潜在参与者的筛选。 制定学习目标 ，以指导项目的设计。 设计学习与发展需求分析调研 。 制定监测流程 ， 以有效跟踪参与者在项目结束后社会影响项目的进展。 按照约定的项目时间表 实施现场培训课程 。 每天举行一次当日总结会议 ，以回顾每日项目实施情况。 对所有参与者进行 前测和 / 或后测问卷 。 开发培训内容和学习旅程 。 邀请并协调演讲嘉宾，以支持计划中的对话环节 。 进行培训需求分析调研 ，以识别参与者面临的需要和挑战。 审核培训内容，以纳入已识别的挑战 。 举行 1 次会议 ，以批准项目和培训内容。 设计并开发前测和 / 或后测问卷 。 分析所有数据，以 评估学习成果 。 起草项目结案报告 。 起草供 CHF 在项目结束后使用的监测流程和模板 。 开发培训师指南（可选） 调研 设计 执行 评估
[slide9] 提议的项目格式整合了不同的学习交付模式 学习旅程 沉浸式学习体验为参与者提供了真实的学习机会，使他们能够亲身体验职场工作与学习的情况。 社区内提供服务项目的人员和服务受益者的参与，将丰富学习成果。 对话 通过对话了解职业成功者的个人经历，将激励并激发参与者效仿这些成功案例。 促进职业成功者和参与者之间的交流，将为参与者在互动过程中增添人脉价值。   关键方法：采用混合式成人学习方法，以平衡不同的需求和学习风格。 培训课程 根据参与者确定的学习需求定制的培训课程，将支持他们学习易于应用的框架和工具，以设计和实施其职业生涯项目。 此外，还将开展每日反思会议，以支持参与者的整个学习过程。
[slide10] Proposed programme overview Day 1 Day 2 Day 3 Day 4 Morning (9am - 12pm) Context Setting &amp; Getting to Know You Learning Journey/ Volunteering Activity Learning Journey/ Volunteering Activity Reflection &amp; Preparation Lunch (12.30pm - 2pm) In Conversation with  In Conversation with In Conversation with - Afternoon (2.30pm - 5.30pm) Training &amp; Reflection Training &amp; Reflection Training &amp; Reflection Social Impact Project Presentation Participants are expected to arrive in Singapore before Day 1. Day 4 will focus the participants on completing the design of their project and preparations for the Presentation. The Presentation segment can be moved to the earlier parts of the day, if required. No training will be delivered on Day 4. Participants are expected to design their social impact projects in the evenings to prepare for their presentation of their social impact project during the closing segment of the programme. This presentation can be designed as a pitch session for different amount of grants to support their implementation or can be designed as a sharing session with key stakeholders as part of a reflect &amp; celebrate event to mark the successful completion of their programme. CHF will provide accommodation and training venue for the programme, if conducted in-person. Empact will be responsible for the design delivery of all contents, as well as outreach and coordinate with community partners to support the learning activities - ‘Context Setting &amp; Getting to Know You’,  ‘In Conversation with’ ,  ‘Learning Journey/ Volunteering Activity’ ,  ‘Training &amp; Reflection’  and ‘Social Impact Project Presentation’. Additionally, Empact will provide project management and coordination support (such as coordination of transportation) to the programme, including provision of lunch (tea break is not provided) for each programme day and transport for activities, where required.
[slide11] Context Setting &amp; Getting to Know You This activity aims to achieve the following objectives: Setting the context and expectations with participants Building the cohort to facilitate sustained engagement of participants post programme (Participants can become alumni who can be called upon to support future cohorts as mentors, speakers or trainers) Understand the UN SDGs and refresh on the social impact causes of CHF Proposed programme flow: Duration (180 mins) Activity Learning Objectives 30 mins Introductions and Programme Overview Participants are aware of the purpose of the programme and the programme activities 120 mins Cohort building activities Participants break the ice, learn of each other and build networks 30 mins Understanding social impact through the lens of CHF Participants learn about UN SDGs and the different CSR focus areas of CHF
[slide12] In Conversation with This activity aims to achieve the following objectives: Inspire and motivate participants to emulate success achieved by speakers and understand the passion that speakers embody for their community Value add to participants network by introducing social impact leaders and changemakers whom they can continue to engage in their social impact journey Potential speakers to invite: Philanthropy: Martin Tan (The Majurity Trust), Usha Pillai (AmberHeart Foundation) Community: Joanna Tan (Children’s Wishing Well), Koh Seng Choon (Project Dignity), Cho Mingxiu (CampusPsy) ChangeMakers: Feature ground-up leaders or employees from CapitaLand who have made significant contributions to CSR efforts Proposed programme flow: Duration (90 mins) Activity Learning Objectives 5 mins Introductions Participants are aware of the purpose of the programme and background of the speakers 40 mins Dialogue with Speakers Participants hear and be inspired by the sharing of speakers 45 mins Networking Lunch Participants gain deeper understanding of speakers’ motivations or the social impact of their work over lunch
[slide13] Learning Journey/ Volunteering Programme This activity aims to achieve the following objectives: Provide authentic learning opportunities for participants to gain first-hand insights on social impact delivery within the community Engage people within the community who delivers service programmes and interact with service users who benefit from these programme to enrich learning outcomes Potential learning journeys (learning journeys can be designed into volunteering programmes, if available): Happiness Initiative  - Participants will hear from one of their co-founders when they participate in a mental wellbeing activity hosted by the organisation. The sharing will seek to inform participants on how the social enterprise was founded, their programmes and their impact. Participants will also learn about the types of volunteer contributions that can be designed to better support the organisation and their service users. Children’s Wishing Well  - Participants will bring children beneficiaries to nearby supermarkets to shop for fresh groceries, important household, and personal care items on a budget of $50 per child. The programme aims to inculcate a healthier eating lifestyle in our children, guide them towards managing their money better as well as develop a greater sense of independence and responsibility. Singapore Fashion Runway  - Participants will engage with neurodiverse youths in learning how to make fashion accessories through basic sewing and crafting. Participants will also learn about their social mission as a social enterprise to empower disadvantaged communities through creative arts, performing acts and entrepreneurship, so that their talents are presented in Singapore Fashion Runway shows/ platforms. CapitaLand Hope Foundation activities  - Participants can also engage with an existing activity by CHF’s grantees.
[slide14] Training &amp; Reflection Sessions 1-2: Social Project Design Learning objectives  Understand corporate planning considerations when designing social impact projects Learn to identify root causes to social problems Learn the Logic Model Framework for application in social impact project design/ review Learn to develop outcome indicators for more effective measurement of impact Sessions 3: Project Management Learning objectives  Learn the key practices in managing a social impact project, including managing employee volunteers and collecting data for measurement of project outcomes Learn good practices in managing social impact projects through facilitated peer learning to hear from fellow participants’ experiences in designing and implementing their social impact projects Project Consultations Participants are expected to design their social impact projects during the programme and prepare a pitch to be presented during the closing segment.  The last 30-45 mins of each Training &amp; Reflection activity will be focused on providing support to participants to address challenges in designing their social impact projects - logic model, outcome indicators development and/ or implementation challenges
[slide15] Social Impact Project Presentation This activity aims to achieve the following objectives: Showcase the learning journey of participants Showcase the social impact projects of participants Celebrate the completion of the programme  Proposed programme flow: Duration (180 mins) Activity Learning Objectives 20 mins Reflection of Participants Journey Overview of participants’ learning journey and key highlights of the programme  140 mins Pitch Presentations Participants to showcase their social impact projects  20 mins Celebrations Celebrate participants completion of the programme
[slide16] Key Consideration for In-person Delivery Advantages Face-to-face learning &amp; interaction activities enriches learning outcomes In-person interactions and shared experience supports building of relationships among participants (cohort building) In-person learning journeys immerse participants in the community to enrich learning outcomes Disadvantages Increases implementation cost as participants require additional budget to travel to SG - Pilot the programme for this region to better manage travel costs Addition of accommodation costs for participants from the region - Leverage on CapitaLand owned residential properties to provide accommodation to participants Note: If virtual implementation is selected, Learning Journeys as a learning format may need to be replaced with alternative learning formats, such as dialogues focusing on providing insights to organisations challenges in service delivery to better inform participants as they design/ review project ideas. Empact will further discuss alternative learning formats, if virtual implementation is selected.
